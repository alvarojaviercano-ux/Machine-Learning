Unsupervised Machine Learning — Concepts

What is Unsupervised Learning?

In Unsupervised Learning, the data does not come with a correct answer. There is no "target" column like in Logistic Regression or KNN. We just give the model the data, and it tries to find groups or patterns on its own.

What is Clustering?

Clustering means putting similar items into the same group, called a cluster, without telling the model what the groups should look like beforehand. Items that are close to each other end up together.

Centroids

A centroid is the "center point" of a cluster — the average position of all the points that belong to it. K-Means keeps moving the centroids until they stop changing.

Iterations

K-Means repeats the same two steps over and over: (1) assign each point to the closest centroid, (2) move each centroid to the average of its points. Each repetition is called an iteration.

How K-Means Works, Step by Step
  1. Choose k, the number of clusters you want to find.
  2. Place k centroids, either randomly or at chosen starting points.
  3. Calculate the distance from every data point to every centroid.
  4. Assign each point to its closest centroid.
  5. Recalculate each centroid as the average of the points assigned to it.
  6. Repeat steps 3–5 until the centroids stop moving (the model has converged).
Why scale the data before clustering?

K-Means measures distance between points, so a variable with big numbers (like app size in MB) can unfairly dominate a variable with small numbers (like a rating from 1 to 5). Scaling both variables to a similar range makes sure both are treated fairly when the distances are calculated.
