K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) — Concepts

What is KNN?

K-Nearest Neighbors is a model that guesses the group of a new item by looking at the most similar items it already knows about, and picking the group that most of them belong to.

Unlike other models, KNN does not learn a formula ahead of time. It just keeps all the training data, and compares new data to it every time it needs to make a guess.

How it works
  1. Measure the distance between the new item and every item we already have.
  2. Pick the k closest items (the "neighbors").
  3. Use the group that most of those neighbors belong to as the answer.
Why we scale the data

Since KNN uses distances, a variable with big numbers (like elevation in meters) could unfairly outweigh a variable with small numbers (like cadence). That's why we scale all the numbers to a similar range before training.

What kind of problems it can solve

KNN can be used for classification (guessing a category, like in this app) and also for regression (guessing a number, by averaging the neighbors instead of voting). It also works fine when there are more than two categories, like in this project.

Advantages
  • Easy to understand and build.
  • No real "training" step — it just stores the data.
  • Works well with more than two categories.
Limitations
  • Can be slow with very large amounts of data.
  • Sensitive to unscaled or irrelevant variables.
  • The result depends a lot on the value of k.
Real-world example

A common use of KNN is in recommendation systems — for example, suggesting movies to a user based on what similar users liked. In this project, we use the same idea to guess a cyclist's skill level based on other cyclists with similar ride data.

Three Classes Instead of Two

This model sorts cyclists into three groups instead of just two: Beginner Intermediate Advanced. KNN works the same way no matter how many groups there are.

Choosing "k"
  • A small k (like 1 – 3) can make the model too sensitive to noise.
  • A big k makes the model smoother, but it may miss small patterns.
  • This app uses k = 5, a common middle-ground choice.
